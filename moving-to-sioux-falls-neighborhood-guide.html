Moving to Sioux Falls: A Neighborhood Guide

By Landon Farley · November 4, 2026 · 2 min read

One of the first questions I hear from people moving to Sioux Falls is, “Where should we live?” The honest answer is that it depends on what you want your days to feel like. I’ve spent my whole life here, so here’s how I describe each part of town to my clients.

East Side: growing and convenient

The east side is one of the fastest-growing parts of the city. You’ll find lots of shopping, restaurants, and family-friendly neighborhoods, so errands and dinner out are almost always close by. It’s a popular pick for people who value convenience.

West Side: established and scenic

Think beautiful parks, mature trees, and established neighborhoods with a close-knit feel. If you picture yourself walking the dog under big shade trees, start your search here.

South Side: newer and family focused

The south side is known for newer developments, highly regarded schools, and room to grow. Buyers who want a newer build or a bigger yard often end up here.

North Side: affordable and accessible

The north side offers great value, easy access to I-29, and a strong sense of community. For first-time buyers who want to stretch their budget, it’s worth a serious look.

Downtown and the Steel District: vibrant and walkable

If you want lofts, local dining, events, and entertainment right outside your door, downtown is the heart of the city, and it’s steps from Falls Park. The Steel District, one of the newest destinations in town, blends modern living, dining, and work space with easy access to the river greenway.

Central Sioux Falls: charming and connected

Historic homes, tree-lined streets, and classic Sioux Falls charm, all close to everything. The Historic District by McKennan Park and the Ralph Rogers area are two of my personal favorites.

Don’t forget the surrounding communities

Many buyers also look at nearby towns such as Brandon, Harrisburg, Tea, and Lennox for a small-town feel with a commute to the city. I’m happy to walk you through those too.

How to choose

  • Commute: Map the drive to work at rush hour before you fall in love with a house.
  • Schools: Check the school district for any address you’re considering.
  • Age and size of home: Established neighborhoods tend to offer character and bigger trees; newer ones offer newer systems and layouts.
  • Lifestyle: Do you want a quiet street, a walkable downtown, or a trail out the back door?

Every block is different, so treat this as a starting point. Tell me what matters most to you and I’ll point you to the neighborhoods that fit. You can also grab my free Welcome Home to Sioux Falls packet, which has a quick neighborhood overview you can keep handy.
