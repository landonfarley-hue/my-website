First-Time Home Buyer Guide for Sioux Falls

By Landon Farley · November 4, 2026 · 2 min read

Buying your first home is exciting, and it can feel like a lot at once. The good news is that the process follows the same steps almost every time. Here’s how it works in Sioux Falls.

1. Get pre-approved first

A pre-approval letter from a lender tells you how much you can borrow and shows sellers you’re a serious, qualified buyer. It also gives you a clear budget, can speed up your closing, and lets you lock in an interest rate. Talk to a couple of lenders and compare. I’m happy to introduce you to local lenders I trust.

2. Know your real budget

Your monthly payment is more than principal and interest. It also includes property taxes, homeowners insurance, and possibly mortgage insurance. Ask your lender for a full monthly estimate. For perspective, South Dakota has no state personal income tax, but property taxes are set locally, so numbers vary by address.

Don’t forget cash for closing costs, which are often a few percent of the purchase price, plus moving costs and a cushion for repairs. It’s also worth asking your lender about first-time buyer and down payment assistance programs, including South Dakota Housing’s new Give Me Five program, which offers 5% down payment help.

3. List your must-haves and nice-to-haves

Bedrooms, garage, yard, commute, school district, and neighborhood feel all matter. Sorting these before you tour keeps you from chasing homes that don’t fit. Not sure which part of town suits you? Start with my neighborhood guide.

4. Search with a strategy

Homes you see online are only part of the picture. I’m an expert in buying and selling off market, which can open doors you won’t find on a public search. Read more in what off-market homes are and how they work.

5. Make a strong offer

Your offer covers price, earnest money, contingencies, and timeline. We’ll look at recent comparable sales so you offer with confidence, not guesswork.

6. Inspect with care

No home is perfect, and a professional inspection is a visual assessment of the property’s major systems and visible condition. Use it to understand what needs attention now, what can wait, and what to negotiate. It isn’t a guarantee, so ask lots of questions.

7. Appraisal, final loan steps, and closing

Your lender will order an appraisal, and your loan goes through final underwriting. Avoid big purchases or new credit during this time. At closing you’ll sign the paperwork, pay your closing costs, and get your keys. I’ll also be there for the final walk-through to make sure everything is as agreed.

Ready to start?

The best first step is a short conversation about your goals and timeline. Let’s talk, and I’ll map out the process for you.
