What the Biggest Real Estate Companies Do for Clients (and What It Means for You)

By Landon Farley · November 4, 2026 · 4 min read

The real estate industry changes fast, and the biggest companies spend a lot of money figuring out what clients want. I looked at what the largest brokerages and busiest agents are doing, and sorted out what’s useful for buyers and sellers here in Sioux Falls and what’s just marketing.

The scale of the big names

  • Keller Williams was ranked the No. 1 U.S. real estate brand by both transaction sides and sales volume in the 2026 RealTrends Verified ranking. Of the top 500 brokerages by 2025 sides, 191 (38.2%) were KW-affiliated, according to KW’s press release. KW describes itself as the world’s largest real estate franchise by agent count. (KW press release)
  • Compass is part of a group of nine brokerage brands with about 340,000 agents, and it signed a three-year listing partnership with Redfin (now owned by Rocket) in early 2026. (Houston Agent Magazine)

Size isn’t the same as service, but the biggest firms are all competing on a few of the same things. Here’s what they are.

1. Early access to homes

Compass “Coming Soon” listings now appear on Redfin with priority placement, and in May 2026 Redfin launched Early Access, a search category for homes listed only on Redfin plus pre-market listings from Compass brands. Buyers get alerts as these homes appear, and sellers can test pricing and demand before a full public launch. (Redfin announcement)

What it means for you: hearing about a home early is valuable, and it’s the reason I focus on off-market and quiet sales here in Sioux Falls.

2. Financing perks

As part of its Redfin deal, Compass says its clients can get a one-year, 1% interest rate reduction or a lender credit of up to $6,000 through Rocket Mortgage. (Houston Agent Magazine) Offers like this change often and come with terms, so read the details.

What it means for you: ask any agent which lenders and programs can lower your costs. In South Dakota, the biggest one right now may be SD Housing’s Give Me Five program.

3. Technology that speeds up service

Keller Williams opened its Command platform to more integrations in February 2026, and the company is adding AI tools for agents. (KW press release) The point of all this technology is faster follow-up and fewer dropped details.

What it means for you: technology should show up as quicker replies and better organized deals. It doesn’t replace judgment. In NAR’s data, what buyers most want from an agent is still the human part: explaining the process, spotting problems, and negotiating. (NAR Magazine)

4. A team behind the agent

Many top producers work in teams, with different people handling different jobs, such as a lead agent, a transaction coordinator, and client relations and marketing. (example from a KW team) The benefit to clients is coverage and fewer things falling through the cracks.

What it means for you: I’m part of a team, so someone is always available when you need an answer.

5. Relationships that last past closing

This is where the most successful agents separate themselves. One industry resource reports that top performers get 65% or more of their business from past clients and referrals, and that winning a new client costs roughly 5 to 7 times more than keeping one. (Rework) NAR’s 2025 survey points the same way: 43% of buyers found their agent through a referral, 18% used an agent they had worked with before, and more than 9 in 10 said they would use their agent again. (Virginia REALTORS® summary of NAR’s 2025 Profile)

What it means for you: the best agents treat you as a client for life, not a single deal. That’s how I want to work.

How top agents get new clients, and what doesn’t work

Referrals and repeat clients lead the way. Social media and video get a lot of attention, but the numbers are more modest than the hype: 39% of REALTORS® said social media gave them their highest number of quality leads, yet only 3% of buyers made first contact with their agent through social media. About 37% of buyers did use online video during their search. (Luxury Presence summary of NAR data)

In other words, content helps people get to know and trust an agent, but trust is what actually wins the business. That’s why this site has market updates, guides and a calculator: to give you something useful, with no pressure.

What matters most when you hire an agent

  • Honest guidance: 54% of buyers said their agent pointed out issues they might have missed. (NAR via Virginia REALTORS®)
  • Help with the process: especially if you’re buying for the first time. 76% of first-time buyers said they valued their agent’s guidance.
  • Good communication: see my post on 8 questions to ask before you hire an agent.
